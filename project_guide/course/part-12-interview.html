12. Explain and defend the project

Say what is true, explain your choices, and name the limits before an interviewer finds them.

A 45-second explanation

“I built a credit-risk prediction system around UCI's 30,000-row Taiwan credit-card default dataset. The target is next-month payment default, so it is default prediction rather than transaction fraud detection. I inspected data quality and class balance, then built a past-only feature pipeline for utilisation, payment ratios, repayment history, averages, trends, and a high-utilisation flag. I compared class-aware logistic regression, random forest, and CatBoost with stratified cross-validation. CatBoost won on cross-validated ROC-AUC. I selected a 0.245 probability threshold on validation F1, then evaluated once on a held-out 6,000-row test set: ROC-AUC 0.7805, average precision 0.5582, precision 0.4941, recall 0.5998, and F1 0.5419. I deployed the saved full pipeline through Flask so training and inference use identical preprocessing.”

Natural presentation flow

  1. State the problem and distinguish default from fraud.
  2. Introduce the UCI data, 30,000 rows, target, and limits.
  3. Describe authentic checks: no nulls/duplicates, 22.12% defaults, recent repayment status strongly associated with target.
  4. Explain past-only features and why pipeline fitting avoids leakage.
  5. Describe the 60/20/20 split and stratified cross-validation on training only.
  6. Name the three candidates and CatBoost selection.
  7. Give metrics, then explain why F1 threshold 0.245 trades precision for recall.
  8. Finish with Flask, saved artifact, aggregate public visuals, and honest limitations.

Questions an interviewer may ask

Why was accuracy insufficient?

Because 77.88% of rows are no default. An always-no-default baseline gets 77.88% accuracy and catches no defaults. I reported recall, precision, F1, ROC-AUC, and average precision.

Why did you use a 0.245 threshold instead of 0.5?

I selected it on the validation set to maximise F1. On final test, 0.245 produced recall 0.5998 and F1 0.5419. At 0.5, precision and accuracy increased but recall fell to 0.3527. In production I would choose the operating threshold with stakeholders using review cost, capacity, and calibration.

How did you prevent leakage?

I split before tuning, put feature engineering/imputation/encoding/scaling inside a sklearn pipeline, fit within cross-validation training folds, used validation for threshold selection, and held test data until final reporting. All engineered inputs use historical months only.

Why CatBoost?

It had the highest mean cross-validated ROC-AUC among the three implemented candidates, 0.7876. It can model nonlinear interactions in tabular data. I would not claim it is universally best, and I would compare more models only if the added complexity is justified.

What would you improve next?

Use time-aware validation if dates are available, validate calibration, report MCC and subgroup/fairness analyses, make threshold decisions cost-aware, add production authentication/rate limits/monitoring, and govern retraining with outcome feedback.

Weak points to volunteer honestly

IssueGood response
Academic historical data“It proves an ML workflow, not production readiness. It lacks current operational and fairness context.”
F1 threshold rule“It is reproducible but does not represent a bank's full cost policy.”
No SMOTE“I used class weighting where configured and threshold analysis. I would test resampling inside folds, not on validation/test.”
No individual customer explanation UI“The project has global SHAP analysis. Individual explanations need careful product, privacy, and fairness design.”

A strong interview answer is specific and calm. Do not pretend EDA caused every design choice or say the model can make autonomous lending decisions. Show that you understand the implementation and its limits.
