Part 1

Default prediction, not transaction fraud

The root app estimates whether a client will default on the next payment. It does not inspect an individual transaction for fraud.

CONCEPTUALclearly labelled context
LIMITATIONnever fabricated

What this means

A feature is an input clue: credit limit, demographics, repayment status, bill or payment. A label/target is the historical answer. This is supervised binary classification: default=1 and no default=0. Rules miss interactions among these signals; a trained model learns patterns from labelled examples. Learning a pattern is not proof of causation, fairness, or suitability for automatic lending.

Start with the everyday picture

Imagine a lender looking at a person who already has a credit card. The lender is not trying to read that person's mind. It has a small record of what happened before: the credit limit, the bills sent over recent months, amounts paid, and whether payments were late. The question in this dataset is very narrow: based on those old records, did the client default on the payment due next month?

"Prediction" does not mean certainty. If the model gives 0.70, it is placing the profile on the high end of its learned risk scale. The current project has not checked whether a 0.70 score is perfectly calibrated, so do not translate that score into a promise that exactly seven out of ten similar people will default.

Why labels matter

A computer cannot learn this problem from inputs alone. It needs past examples where the answer is known. Each old row says, in effect, "this client had these circumstances, and the recorded next-month outcome was 0 or 1." During training, the model looks for input patterns that tend to appear more often before a 1 than before a 0. At prediction time, the target column is absent. Giving it the answer would be cheating.

A careful way to talk about benefit

A risk estimate could help staff decide which accounts to look at first. It cannot tell us why a client may struggle, whether they deserve credit, or what a fair decision would be. In real lending, people need clear policies, review, privacy protection, legal controls, and a chance to correct mistakes. This repository is a learning project that demonstrates the technical path from data to a score.

Check understanding

Try each aloud before opening it: five beginner, five intermediate and two challenging questions.

Beginner — What is the target?

default.payment.next.month: 1 means default, 0 no default.

Beginner — Is this transaction fraud?

No. Root project is next-month client default prediction.

Beginner — What is a feature?

An input clue such as PAY_0 or LIMIT_BAL.

Beginner — Why test data?

To estimate performance on records not used to fit.

Beginner — What limitation matters?

It is historical Taiwan 2005 data, not production lending data.

Intermediate — Evidence for claim?

Cite active root source, paired artifacts, data inspection or recomputed metrics.

Intermediate — What ranks thresholds?

ROC-AUC; active GridSearchCV uses it.

Intermediate — Why can accuracy mislead?

Always predicting no default yields 77.88% accuracy and zero recalls.

Intermediate — What is leakage?

Evaluation/future information influences fitting.

Intermediate — What must accompany model.pkl?

preprocessor.pkl because it produces the expected 42 features.

Challenge — Best improvement?

Select on train-only stratified CV, reserve test, then choose threshold/calibration by cost.

Challenge — Interview summary?

Educational default-risk prototype with evidence-based limits, not a production decision system.
