Evidence-led project walkthrough

How historical credit behaviour becomes a review signal

This application estimates the probability of next-month credit-card payment default using UCI's historical Taiwan client dataset. It is a learning and risk-review prototype. It is not transaction-fraud detection and it does not make lending decisions on its own.

The decision support problem

Why estimate default risk?

A lender may need to decide which accounts deserve an earlier human review. Missing a likely default can increase financial loss. Flagging a person who would pay on time creates unnecessary work and can be unfair. The model gives staff one probability signal from historical patterns. A policy, a person, and safeguards still decide what happens next.

Inputs

Credit limit, demographic source codes, repayment statuses, bills, and payments across six months.

Output

A class-1 probability: observed next-month payment default in the historical dataset.

Boundary

No transaction, merchant, device, or location data exists here. This is not a fraud model.

Feature engineering inside the pipeline

Give the model behaviour, not just columns

The raw dataset provides six months of statuses, bills, and payments. The custom sklearn transformer adds past-only summaries. It runs both during training and during a website prediction, which prevents the two paths from calculating different inputs.

Capacity and utilisation

BILL_AMT1 / LIMIT_BAL places the newest bill beside the available credit limit. A 50,000 bill means something different under a 60,000 limit than under a 500,000 limit.

Payment behaviour

Six payment-to-bill ratios, mean repayment status, maximum delay, delayed-month count, and early-payment flag turn a monthly sequence into understandable summaries.

Level and direction

Average bills, average payments, and linear trends describe typical balances and whether bills or payments were rising across the observed history.

Leakage safeguard: every engineered value uses only historical fields supplied before the target month. Imputation, one-hot encoding, and standard scaling are fitted inside the pipeline on training folds, never calculated from the final test set.
Training design

Three models were compared before one was served

The raw records are split with stratification: 18,000 training rows, 6,000 validation rows, and 6,000 untouched test rows. The training partition uses three-fold stratified cross-validation and a compact GridSearchCV search. ROC-AUC selects the candidate; validation F1 selects the operating threshold.

raw CSV→60/20/20 split→pipeline fit in training folds→compare candidates→validation threshold→one final test

Final-test evaluation

What the selected model gets right and wrong

The deployed review threshold is {{ "{:.1%}".format(summary.model.threshold) }}, selected by validation F1. It is lower than 0.50 because this prototype values catching more known defaults, while accepting more review flags. A real organisation would choose its threshold with review capacity, costs, fairness, and calibration evidence.

Model explanation

Inspect model behaviour without claiming cause

Careful interpretation

Repayment status, delay summaries, utilisation, and payment amounts can influence this trained model. That does not make any feature a complete explanation of a client's future, and it does not replace fairness analysis, legal review, human oversight, or an appeal route.

Production work still needed

Authentication, rate limiting, drift monitoring, outcome feedback, threshold governance, calibration validation, and subgroup/fairness assessment are not implemented in this academic prototype.
